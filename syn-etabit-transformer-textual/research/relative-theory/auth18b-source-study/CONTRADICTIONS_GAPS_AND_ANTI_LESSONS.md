# Contradictions, gaps and anti-lessons

## Open gaps

- Substantive primary Government-and-Binding text was not available in the bounded study.
- The selected WordNet sample contains no `(p)`, `(ip)`, participial `<` or attribute `=` example.
- The proposed generic vocabulary has not been adopted, versioned in Etabit or tested against a live map API.
- The study does not establish how multiple theory drives arbitrate conflicts.

## Anti-lessons

- Do not turn a publisher's list of topics into formal rules.
- Do not use the word “relation” as evidence that WordNet, grammar theory and project graph edges mean the same thing.
- Do not erase lexical-versus-semantic pointer scope when normalizing links.
- Do not infer a root or affix merely because a character sequence is recognizable.
- Do not let a convenient generic edge replace the original source symbol or source locator.
- Do not call a metadata-only source “reviewed” at the substantive level.
- Do not treat an offline inspector, hash manifest or passing local tests as independent linguistic attestation.
- Do not make a theory map privileged: the design requires explicit contextual selection and replaceability.

The conservative result is useful: uncertainty is represented as an addressable record instead of being lost inside code.
